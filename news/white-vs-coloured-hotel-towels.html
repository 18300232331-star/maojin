White vs Coloured Hotel Towels: Which Colour to Order for Your Hotel

2026-11-08 · Product Knowledge

White is the right order for most hotels: it can be bleached back to bright, it matches any room, and one colour keeps the laundry simple. Coloured towels suit hotels that want a brand look or need to hide stains — but the colour has to survive the hotel laundry, so confirm the dyeing and the wash care before ordering, not after.

1. Why white is still the hotel default

Walk into almost any hotel room, in almost any country, and the towels are white. That is not habit alone — white solves three problems that every hotel laundry faces.

  • White can be bleached back to bright. Hotel laundry is hard on towels: hot water, strong detergent, dozens of wash cycles. White towels can go through a bleach cycle and come out bright again. That one fact is why white stays the default — the laundry has a way to recover a towel that has started to look tired.
  • White matches every room. A white towel fits a white bathroom, a dark bathroom, a beige bathroom and a tiled bathroom. There is no colour clash to manage, no need to match the towel to the room design, and no problem when a room is redecorated — the same towels still fit.
  • White keeps the laundry simple. One colour means one wash stream. There is no sorting by colour, no risk of a dark towel bleeding onto a light one, and no separate wash settings for different colours. For a laundry that processes hundreds of towels a day, that simplicity is worth money.

White has one honest drawback: it shows every stain. A makeup mark, a tea stain, a scuff — all of it is visible on white. Hotels accept that because the bleach cycle deals with most of it, and because a stain that will not come out is a clear signal to retire the towel. The visibility is also a kind of quality control: a white towel that still looks white after washing is a towel the guest trusts.

2. When white starts to cost more than it saves

White is the default, but it is not free. Two situations make buyers look at colour.

First, stain-heavy properties: spa hotels, beach resorts, family hotels and properties where guests use towels for makeup, sun cream or food. In those properties the bleach cycle runs constantly, and towels retire early on stains. A coloured towel hides the light stains that would retire a white towel, so the towel stays in service longer.

Second, brand-led properties: boutique hotels, resorts and chains with a strong colour identity. A towel in the brand colour — on the bed, in the bathroom, by the pool — is part of the room design, not just a utility item. For those buyers the towel is a design choice, and white is not the look they want.

If neither of those applies — a standard city hotel, a business hotel, a budget chain — white is almost always the cheaper choice over the life of the towel, because the laundry can keep it bright with the bleach cycle it already runs.

3. Coloured towels: what to check before ordering

A coloured towel is only a good order if the colour survives the hotel laundry. A colour that fades after a few washes is worse than white: the towels look old, they no longer match each other, and the property ends up replacing them early. Check these four points before ordering, not after the first wash.

  • Ask how the colour is dyed. Reactive dyeing bonds the colour to the cotton fibre and holds up far better in hot hotel washes than surface dyeing. Ask the supplier which dyeing method is used for the colour you want, and ask for a sample in that exact colour before the bulk order.
  • Ask whether the colour can take the hotel wash. Tell the supplier your wash conditions — hot water, detergent type, whether you bleach — and ask whether the colour is made for them. A colour that is fine in a home wash can fade fast in a hotel wash. Samples before bulk production are the way to find out.
  • Order the whole set in the same dye lot. Towels dyed in different batches can differ slightly in shade, and the difference shows once they are folded together in a room. Order bath towels, hand towels and face towels in the same colour and the same production run, so the set matches.
  • Mind the minimum per colour. Coloured towels are dyed per colour, so the minimum order applies per colour: bath towels 300 pieces per colour and hotel series 500 pieces per colour. A hotel that wants three colours is ordering three minimums, not one — so most hotels pick one brand colour and keep the rest white.

4. The mixed order most hotels actually place

Few hotels order all white or all coloured. The common pattern is a mix, and it is worth copying if you have not settled the question.

  • White for the room towels. Bath towels, hand towels and face towels in the guest bathroom stay white — they get the hardest use, the most stains, and the most washes, and the bleach cycle keeps them presentable.
  • Colour for the accent pieces. Pool towels, beach towels, spa towels and gym towels carry the brand colour. They are used once a day, washed less aggressively, and seen by guests as a design item rather than a utility item.
  • Colour for the logo, not the towel. An embroidered or jacquard logo in the brand colour on a white towel gives the brand look without dyeing the whole towel. It keeps the bleach-friendly white base and adds the colour where it shows. Ask at inquiry which logo methods suit the towel and colour you have in mind.

This mix also keeps the order simple: one white minimum for the room set, one colour minimum for the accent pieces, and no three-colour multiplication of minimums.

5. Ordering the colour: samples first, then bulk

Colour is the one towel choice that cannot be judged from a photo. A screen shows a colour differently from a laundry room, and a swatch in daylight looks different again under bathroom light. Order in this sequence.

  • Ask for a sample in the exact colour. Samples before bulk production are part of the ordering process. Wash the sample in your own laundry, the way you wash guest towels, and look at it after several washes — not just when it arrives.
  • Check the sample against the room. Put the washed sample in a real guest bathroom, under the real light, next to the tiles and fittings. A colour that looks right in the office can look wrong in the room.
  • Confirm the minimum and the lead time per colour. The US$500 minimum order applies including samples, and dyeing a colour takes its own production time. Confirm both at inquiry, and confirm that the full set — bath, hand and face towels — can be dyed in the same run.

General guidance on cotton home products notes that towel quality is read from fibre, yarn, pile and GSM together, not from colour alone. The lesson for the colour choice is the same: colour is a laundry decision first and a design decision second, and the colour your laundry can keep is the colour to order.

Frequently asked questions

Do white hotel towels really last longer than coloured ones? In a hotel laundry, usually yes — because white can go through the bleach cycle that restores brightness, while a coloured towel that fades cannot be restored. The difference is the laundry, not the cotton: the same towel in white and in colour ages differently in the wash.

Will coloured towels fade in a hotel wash? It depends on the dyeing method and on your wash conditions. Reactive-dyed colours hold up far better in hot hotel washes than surface-dyed colours. Tell the supplier your wash conditions and ask for a sample in the exact colour, then wash it your way before ordering in bulk.

Can I order white room towels and coloured pool towels together? Yes — that is the most common mixed order. The white room set and the coloured accent pieces are separate minimums, so confirm the minimum per colour at inquiry and order the set in one production run so the colours match.

Why do hotels not just order dark towels to hide all stains? Dark towels hide stains, but they also hide the signs a guest reads as clean — and they cannot be bleached. A dark towel with a stain the guest can see is still a stained towel, and a faded dark towel looks older than a bright white one. Most hotels use colour as an accent, not as a hiding place.

What should I send with a towel colour inquiry? The colour you want (with a reference if you have one), the towel types and quantities per colour, your wash conditions, and whether you want a logo in the brand colour. Ask for a sample in the exact colour first, and wash it your way before confirming the bulk order.
