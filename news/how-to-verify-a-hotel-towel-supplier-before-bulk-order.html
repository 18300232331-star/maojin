Hotel Towel Supplier Verification: How to Check a Supplier Before Your First Bulk Order

2026-11-28 · Product Knowledge

Before your first bulk hotel towel order, verify the supplier in three steps: check the business and how it answers, order samples and test them for size, weight, stitching and colour, and get the MOQ, price, lead time and payment terms in writing. A supplier who sends a real sample, answers plain questions directly and puts the terms in writing is usually safe to order from; one who cannot do those three things is not.

1. First, check the supplier itself — before you talk about towels

A towel is easy to photograph well; a supplier is harder to fake, and the checks here cost nothing but a few emails. Start with the basics: a real business name, a real address, and a range that matches what you want. A hotel towel seller should show sizes, weights, colours and order quantities on its own site, not just a chat handle and a price list — if it lists none of those, you would be ordering blind.

Then watch how the supplier answers: send three plain questions in one message — sizes and weights, minimum order per colour, how long a first order takes. A good supplier answers all three directly, with numbers; one who answers only the price, changes the subject, or takes a week to reply will not get faster or clearer once your money is paid.

Ask how they handle a problem order — a short count, a wrong colour, a late shipment. You need a process, not a promise problems never happen. Check the contact details work: write to the published email address, not only to a chat account, and see whether a real person replies. Our contact email is zzq@huosidun.com.cn. If the only way to reach a supplier is a chat app with no email, no address and no website, keep your first order small — or keep looking.

2. Order samples — and test them like a buyer

Never place a first bulk order from photos alone: they show colour and pile, not weight, hand feel, stitching or how a towel behaves after washing. Order samples of the exact towel you plan to buy — same size, same weight, same colour — and test them as your housekeeping team will use them. Samples are part of the order here: every order meets the US$500 minimum, including samples, with no exceptions.

When the samples arrive, check 5 things. First, size: lay it flat, smooth the folds, and measure length and width. A bath towel is 70×140 cm and a bath sheet 80×160 cm in our hotel towel sizes guide — a towel 5 cm short will not fold to the size your shelves are set for. Second, weight: weigh it on a gram scale (see section 3). Third, stitching: check hems, selvedges and corners, and pull gently at a hem. Fourth, colour: compare it with the colour you asked for in daylight. Fifth, absorbency: run the 3-second sink test in our absorbency testing: the 3-second sink test on every batch guide.

Keep the sample that passes as the standard the bulk order is judged against — our five checks before you approve a towel sample guide covers that approval step. If a supplier will not send a sample, or sends one that does not match the size and weight quoted, stop there: no sample, no order.

3. The GSM spot check: weigh the sample

GSM means grams per square metre: higher GSM means a denser, heavier towel. CottonWorks puts towels in two bands — 400–600 GSM lighter and faster drying, 600–800 GSM plush and luxurious. Daily washing suits the lighter band; a 5-star bathroom may want the plush one.

Then check the sample with a scale: a 70×140 cm bath towel measures 0.98 square metres, so at 600 GSM it should weigh 588 g — arithmetic from the live product page's own published spec for our hotel bath towel 70 by 140 cm. At 500 GSM the same towel should weigh about 490 g; that is a worked example, not a product spec. Weigh 5 towels and average them. Clearly below the implied weight means a thinner towel than quoted.

Weigh dry towels only: cotton holds water by nature, and published tables give its standard commercial moisture regain as 8.5% at 65% RH and 20°C, so a towel in normal air already carries about 8.5% of its dry weight as water. A damp sample reads heavy and flatters a thin towel. See our GSM explained: what 400, 500 and 600 really mean guide and the hotel bath towel weight chart for how weight bands feel in use.

4. Stitching, colour and absorbency: what to look for on the sample

Weight tells you how dense a towel is; stitching, colour and absorbency tell you how long it will last. Start with stitching: open the sample fully and look at both faces, the hems, the selvedges and the corners for loose hemming, skipped stitches, pulled loops and uneven pile. A hem already coming undone will come undone faster after 100 hotel washes. Pull gently at a hem: stitching that holds is a good sign; stitching that opens is a reason to stop.

Then colour: lay the sample flat in daylight and check the dye is even — no pale patches, streaks or darker edges — and check every colour you ordered, since dye lots vary. If the towels carry a logo, check its placement too: a logo 2 cm off centre shows on every towel.

Ask about the dye method and the wash results. Our reactive-dyed colours survive 100 hotel washes — see our reactive dyeing: colour that survives 100 hotel washes guide. Ask for the wash-fastness and rubbing-fastness results from the actual production lot, in writing with the sample. Finally, absorbency: more than 8 in 10 shoppers say cotton is their first choice for sheets, towels and bedding, according to CottonWorks, because cotton absorbs water and washes clean of odour while synthetics repel moisture. Run the 3-second sink test: a towel that floats and stays dry is not doing its job.

5. MOQ and the minimum order: what to expect before you order

MOQ means minimum order quantity: it decides whether a supplier can serve you at all. Ask it early, per colour and per size, not as one vague total — 500 pieces per colour quotes very differently from 500 pieces across all colours. Here a bath towel is 300 pieces per colour as standard, the hotel series 500, and any custom-dyed product 500 — see our hotel towel minimum order quantity guide.

Do the arithmetic before you ask for a price: 3 colours of bath towel at 300 pieces per colour is 900 pieces; 2 colours of the hotel series at 500 is 1,000 pieces. If that is more than you need, ask what fits: a smaller first order, fewer colours, or a stock colour instead of a custom-dyed one. Every order here meets the US$500 minimum, including samples, with no exceptions, so check your quantities clear both the per-colour MOQ and the US$500 minimum before you confirm. Also ask whether the MOQ covers mixed sizes, and whether a custom dye costs more. Price, freight and lead time: confirm at inquiry.

6. Payment terms, lead time, packing and shipping: get it in writing

Terms are where first orders go wrong: ask for everything in writing, in one order confirmation, before you pay anything. Start with payment: deposit amount, when the balance is due, what document you receive before you pay the balance. Check the account name matches the business name from section 1 — if not, stop and ask why before paying.

Then lead time: days for production from order confirmation, and days for shipping to your port — two separate numbers. One number for "delivery", without saying where production ends and shipping begins, is not a lead time you can plan around. Our lead times depend on product and quantity, so confirm at inquiry.

Then packing: cartons, inner bags, labels on each carton, pieces per carton. Our how we pack 500 bath towels for sea freight guide shows how cartons should look when they leave the factory — sealed, inner bags intact, labels matching the packing list. Ask whether the freight quote covers the goods alone or shipping to your port, and ask for the packing list before shipping. Keep the whole confirmation in one email thread — spec, quantities, price, lead time, payment terms, packing. A supplier who will not put the terms in writing has not agreed to them.

7. Red flags: when to walk away

Most bad first orders show a warning before the money moves: one flag means slow down; two or more means walk away.

First, no sample: a supplier who will not send a sample of the exact towel you want — or sends a different size or weight and calls it "almost the same" — cannot be verified. Second, nothing in writing and no business to check: no spec sheet, MOQ, lead time or payment terms on paper, no address, email or website — only chat messages and a chat handle. Third, a price far below every other quote with no explanation: it usually means a lighter towel, a shorter size, or a specification that will change after payment. Fourth, pressure to pay in full immediately, or only by a method you cannot trace: a deposit and a balance against documents is the normal shape of a first order. Fifth, the sample does not match the quote: 600 GSM quoted, a sample that weighs like 400 GSM.

Trust the pattern, not the promise: one late reply is a busy day, but evasive answers, no sample, no terms in writing and a price too good to be true is a supplier telling you who it is.

8. Your pre-order decision: order, or walk away

Run through this list before you pay anything. Business: real name, address, email that answers, a range that matches hotel towels. Sample: size, weight, stitching, colour in daylight, absorbency. Quantities: per-colour MOQ and the US$500 minimum both clear — bath towel 300, hotel series 500, custom-dyed 500 pieces per colour. Terms: price, lead time, payment, packing, all in one confirmation. If every box is ticked, place the first order, starting with the smallest order that meets the MOQ.

If any box is not ticked, do not pay yet: ask the missing question once, in writing, and give the supplier a day or two to answer. One who fills the gap is one you can work with; one who cannot is one you have just verified.

Verification does not end at the order: once the goods ship, the next check is delivery inspection — our hotel towel delivery inspection checklist guide covers counting, weighing and checking the bulk delivery on arrival. Frequently asked questions

Should I order samples before my first bulk hotel towel order? Yes — always. A sample is the only way to check size, weight, stitching, colour and absorbency before you commit to hundreds of pieces. Order the exact towel you plan to buy, test it against sections 2 to 4, and keep the one that passes. Samples are part of the order here, inside the US$500 minimum, with no exceptions.

How do I check GSM without a lab? Weigh the sample: a 70×140 cm bath towel is 0.98 square metres, so at 600 GSM it should weigh 588 g, and at 500 GSM about 490 g. Weigh 5 dry towels and average them. Weigh dry samples only: cotton already carries about 8.5% of its dry weight as water.

What MOQ should I expect for hotel towels? Ask per colour and per size, not as one total. Here a bath towel is 300 pieces per colour as standard, the hotel series 500, and any custom-dyed product 500. Every order also meets the US$500 minimum, including samples, with no exceptions. Three colours of bath towel is 900 pieces.

What payment terms should I ask about? Ask the deposit amount, when the balance is due, what document you receive before you pay the balance, and whether the account name matches the business name you verified — all in one written confirmation before you pay. Payment terms, price, freight and lead time: confirm at inquiry.

How is supplier verification different from sample approval and delivery inspection? Three checks at three moments: supplier verification (this guide) comes before the first order and decides whether to order at all; sample approval comes before bulk production and decides whether production may start — see our five checks before you approve a towel sample guide; delivery inspection comes on arrival and decides whether to sign for a finished shipment — see our hotel towel delivery inspection checklist guide.
