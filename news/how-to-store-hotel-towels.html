How to Store Hotel Towels: Linen Room Tips That Keep Towels Fresh

2025-10-25 · Product Knowledge

Store hotel towels clean, fully dry, folded loosely on ventilated shelves, with the oldest stock used first. That is the whole method: dry, aired, rotated. Get those three right and towels stay fresh between washes; get any one wrong and even good towels go musty, flat or grey before their time.

1. Why storage matters as much as washing

Hotels spend their attention on washing — temperature, detergent, bleach — and then undo the work in storage. A towel that leaves the laundry clean and dry can still come back to the guest smelling musty if it sat damp in a closed cupboard, or feel flat and thin if it spent weeks crushed under a heavy stack.

Storage damage is slow and easy to miss. Nobody notices the first week. By the time guests complain about smell or the towels look grey, the cause is usually not the wash at all — it is the shelf. The fix is cheaper than a new order: change how the linen room holds the towels, and the same towels last longer and look better.

Think of storage as the second half of the laundry process. Washing cleans the towel; storage keeps it clean until the guest uses it. A hotel that gets both halves right replaces towels less often, and the towels it does replace are replaced for honest wear, not for a smell that storage caused.

2. Dry before shelving: the one rule that decides everything

Never shelve a towel that is still damp. This is the single most important rule in this article, and breaking it causes most storage problems. A damp towel in a stack stays damp: the towels around it trap the moisture, air cannot reach the middle of the pile, and the result is the musty smell guests notice first and forgive least.

Check dryness by feel, not by time. A towel that looks dry on the outside can still be damp in the folded middle, where the pile is thickest. Open one towel from each batch and feel the centre fold before the batch goes on the shelf. If it feels cool or heavy, it needs more drying time — send it back through the dryer or the drying line rather than shelving it and hoping.

In humid climates and rainy seasons this check matters twice as much, because towels take longer to dry and the linen room air is already damp. Keep a small fan or a dehumidifier running in the linen room if the air feels heavy; the point is to dry the room as well as the towels. A dry room keeps dry towels dry.

3. Shelving and airflow: let the towels breathe

Towels need air around them, even on the shelf. Stack them loosely, with space above each stack and gaps between stacks, so air can move through the shelves. A shelf packed tight to the ceiling looks tidy and stores more, but it is a closed box — and a closed box is where musty smells start.

Use open shelving where possible: slatted shelves, wire racks, or shelves with gaps at the back. Avoid solid cupboards with tight doors for everyday stock; reserve closed storage for sealed backup stock, not for the towels housekeeping reaches for every day. If the linen room has a window or a vent, keep it open or running — moving air is the cheapest freshness tool a hotel owns.

Keep towels off the floor. Even a clean floor is the dampest and dustiest place in the room, and a stack that touches the floor picks up both. The bottom shelf should sit well above floor level, and nothing should be stored directly on the floor, even for a night.

Keep the linen room for linen. Cleaning chemicals, mops and wet floor cloths do not belong on the same shelves as guest towels — their smells transfer, and a spill ruins a whole stack. If space is tight, put a solid divider between linen and cleaning supplies, and never stack chemical bottles above towels.

4. Rotation: first in, first out

A hotel that always takes towels from the top of the pile wears out the same few towels while the rest sit ageing on the shelf. The fix is rotation: first in, first out. New stock goes to the back or the bottom of the shelf; housekeeping takes from the front or the top. Every towel in the property gets used in turn, so wear spreads evenly across the whole stock.

Rotation also protects the par stock. Our par-stock guide explains how many sets a hotel should hold; rotation is what makes that stock actually work. Without it, the hotel holds three sets on paper but uses one set in practice — and that one set wears out three times as fast while the other two sit gathering dust.

Make rotation easy to follow. Label shelves by delivery date, or use a simple colour tag on each batch. Housekeeping should not have to think about which stack is oldest — the shelf should show it. When a new delivery arrives, it goes behind the existing stock, not on top of it.

5. What not to do

A short list of the mistakes that cost hotels the most towels:

  • Do not seal fresh towels in plastic bags for daily storage. Sealed bags trap any remaining moisture, and a towel that was nearly dry becomes a damp towel inside the bag. Keep bags for transport and for sealed backup stock only.
  • Do not stack towels higher than the shelf. An over-high stack crushes the towels at the bottom, flattens the pile, and topples onto the floor. If the stack keeps growing, the hotel needs another shelf, not a taller pile.
  • Do not store white and coloured towels pressed together when either is damp. Colour can transfer from a damp coloured towel to a white one, and the stain is hard to wash out. Keep colours apart until both are fully dry.
  • Do not ignore a smell. A musty shelf is a warning, not a nuisance. Find the damp towels, rewash them, dry the shelf, and check the airflow before the smell spreads to the whole stock.

Frequently asked questions

Why do hotel towels smell musty even after washing? Usually the cause is storage, not the wash. A towel shelved while still damp stays damp inside the stack, and the trapped moisture turns musty. Rewash the affected towels, dry them fully — check the centre fold by feel — and improve the airflow in the linen room before shelving the next batch.

How should hotel towels be folded for storage? Fold them loosely, in a size that fits the shelf without squeezing. The fold matters less than the dryness and the air around the stack: a loosely folded, fully dry towel on a ventilated shelf stays fresh, while a tightly folded damp one goes musty whatever the fold.

Can hotel towels be stored in plastic bags? Only for transport and for sealed backup stock. Daily stock should sit on open, ventilated shelves, because a bag traps any remaining moisture. If a bagged towel smells when opened, it was bagged before it was fully dry.

How do I stop white towels going grey in storage? Keep white and coloured towels apart until both are fully dry, so colour cannot transfer from a damp coloured towel. Our white-towels-turning-grey guide covers the laundry causes; storage is the other half — dry, separated, ventilated.

How long can hotel towels sit on the shelf? There is no fixed number — it depends on the room. In a dry, ventilated linen room with proper rotation, towels hold their freshness for a long time. In a damp, still room, even a week can be too long. Rotation is the control: if every towel gets used in turn, none sits long enough to go stale.
