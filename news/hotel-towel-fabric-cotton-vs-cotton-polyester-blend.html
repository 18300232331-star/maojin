Cotton vs Cotton-Polyester Blend Hotel Towels: Which Fabric to Order

2026-11-24 · Product Knowledge

For a hotel bath towel, 100% cotton absorbs the most water and feels the softest on first touch, while a cotton-polyester blend dries faster, shrinks less and keeps its shape through more laundry cycles. The trade is absorbency against laundry life: cotton takes up 8.5% of its dry weight in moisture from the air, against 0.4% for polyester, so every point of polyester in the blend gives up a little absorbency to gain durability. If guests judge the towel by first touch, order cotton; if the laundry judges it by how many cycles it survives, a blend earns its place.

1. What the two fibres do in a towel

Cotton is a plant fibre made of cellulose, with a hollow channel down the middle of each fibre. Water goes inside the fibre itself, which is why a cotton towel can take a lot of water off the skin and still feel dry to the touch until it is quite wet — and also why a soaked cotton towel stays heavy and wet for a long time. Polyester is a manufactured fibre, a synthetic polymer. Water cannot get inside it, so liquid stays on the surface of the fibres, spreads through the gaps between them and evaporates. A polyester towel dries fast but holds almost no water inside the fibre.

The standard way to compare them is moisture regain: the water a dry fibre takes up from the air, as a percentage of its dry weight, measured at 20 °C and 65% relative humidity. These are standard trade values, not our product specs.

FibreStandard moisture regainWhat it means in a towel
100% cotton8.5%Absorbs water into the fibre; soft, cool feel; dries slowly
100% polyester0.4%Absorbs almost no water; liquid spreads on the surface and evaporates; dries fast
60/40 cotton-polyester blendAbout 5.3%Between the two: some absorbency kept, noticeably faster drying than pure cotton

A blend is not a trick and not a defect — it is a mix chosen on purpose. The cotton in it does the absorbing; the polyester in it does the holding-shape and drying. The question is never "which fibre is better" in general. It is "which mix fits this towel, this laundry and this guest". Our guide on why combed cotton costs more explains the cotton side of the choice: combing removes short fibres, so the towel surface is smoother and sheds less lint — worth it where first touch decides the booking.

2. The blend ratios buyers actually meet

In published buying guides for home textiles and towel yarn, most cotton-polyester programmes settle on one of four ratios. These are common trade ratios, not our product specs — always ask for the fibre content in writing, because two towels both sold as a "blend" can be 80% cotton or 50% cotton, and they behave very differently.

Blend (cotton : polyester)Cotton shareWhere published guides place it
80/2080%Everyday towels, hotels and gyms — good absorbency with faster drying than pure cotton
70/3070%Hospitality bath and spa towels — softness plus wash durability
65/3565%Budget commercial and outdoor use — very fast drying, lower cost
50/5050%High-volume commercial laundry — shape holding and abrasion resistance matter most

The pattern is simple: the higher the cotton share, the closer the towel feels and absorbs like pure cotton; the higher the polyester share, the faster it dries and the longer it holds its shape. A 70/30 or 80/20 blend is the usual middle ground for a hotel bath towel, because it keeps most of the cotton feel while cutting drying time and shrinkage. A 50/50 blend belongs where the towel is washed every day and turned around fast, not where a guest expects a thick, soft wrap after a shower.

3. Drying time: what a laboratory test shows

How much faster is a blend, really? A 2025 laboratory study of fabric drying behaviour tested three swatches under the same controlled conditions — 100% cotton, 100% polyester, and a 65/35 polyester-cotton blend — and timed how long each took to dry. These are laboratory fabric swatches, not towels: a real towel's drying time also depends on its GSM, pile height and airflow. But the order is clear, and it holds in both dry and humid air.

Air humidity100% polyester65/35 blend100% cotton
Low humidity (about 20%)About 20 minutesAbout 30 minutesAbout 32 minutes
Humid airUp to 65 minutesAbout 78 minutesAbout 80 minutes

In the same study, cotton dried 11.94 minutes slower than the blend, while polyester dried 12.70 minutes faster than the blend. Two things follow for a buyer. First, the blend is only a little faster than pure cotton in the lab — the big drying gain comes from the towel's weight and pile, not from the fibre alone, so a heavy 600 GSM blend towel will still dry slowly. Second, humid air punishes every fibre: drying times roughly double or triple for all three, which is why a hotel in a humid climate should weigh drying time more heavily than a hotel in a dry one. 4. Shrinkage, shape and laundry life

Drying time is only half the laundry story. The other half is what repeated washing does to the towel's size and shape.

Published typical ranges put shrinkage after five washes at 3–5% for cotton and 0.5–1.5% for polyester, with blends sitting between the two. Cotton fibres swell when wet and the yarns relax from the tension of spinning and finishing, so an untreated cotton towel loses a noticeable amount of length and width in its first washes. Polyester shrinks very little at normal washing temperatures and resists creasing, which is why a blend comes out of the dryer looking closer to the size it went in. Check size after washing, not before: our five checks before you approve a towel sample guide lists size after washing first among the five checks, for exactly this reason.

Strength points the same way. Polyester fibre is stronger and more abrasion-resistant than cotton, and cotton is weaker when wet — the state a towel is in every time it is used. That is why a blend usually survives more laundry cycles before the pile thins and the edges fray. One trade to know: both fibres can pill, but cotton pills tend to break off while polyester pills are held by strong fibres and stay on the surface, so a high-polyester towel can look rougher over time even while it stays strong.

Weight bands matter alongside fibre. In published towel guides, medium GSM of 300–500 is the balanced band for most household and hotel towels, while heavyweight above 500 GSM, up to 800+ GSM for luxury towels, feels thick and plush but takes longer to dry and to launder. A 600 GSM towel in a blend will still dry slowly — fibre choice softens the weight penalty, it does not erase it.

5. Which fabric for which towel

No single fabric wins for every towel in a hotel. Match the fabric to the job the towel does, and to how often it is washed.

TowelFirst choice (buying guidance)Why
Bath towel100% cotton, or an 80/20 blendAbsorbency and softness matter most; the guest wraps in it once a day
Hand towel80/20 or 70/30 blendDries between guests and holds its shape through daily washing
Face towel100% cottonSoftest against the skin; small size means drying time barely matters
Pool towel65/35 or 50/50 blendWashed daily, dried in the sun, turned around fast — drying speed wins
Gym or salon towelBlendFrequent washing and fast turnaround; see our microfibre vs cotton towels for gyms and salons guide for the third option

Sizes do not change with the fabric: a bath towel is still 70×140 cm and a bath sheet 80×160 cm in our hotel towel sizes guide. What changes is how that size behaves after 50 washes — and that is the number to ask about before ordering, not after the first delivery.

6. What to put in your inquiry

Write the fabric down the same way you would write the size. An inquiry that says "hotel bath towels" leaves the mill free to supply any fibre mix; an inquiry that says "80% cotton / 20% polyester, 500–600 GSM, 70×140 cm, bath towel, hotel laundry" can be quoted, sampled and checked. Ask for the fibre content in writing on the quotation and on the sample ticket, and ask for the wash-fastness and rubbing-fastness results with the sample — fastness results vary by shade and construction, so ask for the results rather than a single number.

Frequently asked questions

Is a cotton-polyester blend towel less absorbent than 100% cotton? Yes. Cotton takes up 8.5% of its dry weight in moisture, polyester 0.4%, and a 60/40 cotton-polyester blend about 5.3%. An 80/20 blend keeps most of cotton's absorbency; a 50/50 blend gives up about half of it. If absorbency is the first requirement, order cotton or a high-cotton blend.

Which blend ratio is best for hotel towels? There is no single best ratio. For a bath towel, 80/20 or 70/30 is the usual middle ground — most of the cotton feel with faster drying and less shrinkage. For towels washed every day and turned around fast, such as pool towels, 65/35 or 50/50 fits better. Ask for the fibre content in writing before you approve the sample.

Do blend towels really dry faster? Yes, but only a little faster than pure cotton at the same weight. In a 2025 laboratory test, a 65/35 blend swatch dried in about 30 minutes against about 32 minutes for cotton in dry air, and about 78 minutes against about 80 minutes in humid air. The bigger drying gain comes from choosing a lighter GSM, not from the fibre alone.

Do cotton towels shrink more than blends? Yes. Published typical ranges are 3–5% shrinkage after five washes for cotton, against 0.5–1.5% for polyester, with blends in between. Check the size after washing — not before — and confirm it on the sample before bulk production.

What should I write in my inquiry? The fibre content (for example "80% cotton / 20% polyester"), the GSM band, the size, the quantity per colour and the use — hotel laundry, spa or pool. Then confirm the order against the minimums: 500 pieces per colour for any custom-dyed product, inside the US$500 minimum order. Price, freight and lead time are confirmed at inquiry.
