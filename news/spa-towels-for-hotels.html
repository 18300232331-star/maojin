Spa Towels for Hotels: Sizes, GSM and Care

2026-11-16 · Product Knowledge

A spa towel is not a bath towel used in a smaller room. It meets massage oils, lotions and creams, it is washed after every single treatment, and the guest judges the whole spa by how it feels on the skin. Order the towel by the treatment, not by the room — a soft towel in the right sizes, in a colour that survives daily washing, with enough stock to rotate — and the spa stays presentable every session.

1. What makes a spa towel different

A bath towel is used once a day and washed once a day. A spa towel is used every hour and washed every hour, often with oils and creams that ordinary laundry does not fully remove. That is the whole difference, and it decides everything else: the towel must stay soft after many more washes, it must not hold oil smells, and it must dry fast enough to be back in the treatment room for the next guest.

Softness is the guest's first test. A spa towel touches bare skin for an hour at a time, so a rough towel is felt at once and remembered. Order the softest hand your budget allows for the treatment room, and keep the rougher, harder-wearing towels for the pool and the gym, where nobody lies on them for an hour.

The second test is the laundry. Oils and creams build up in the fibres wash after wash, and a towel that is not washed hot enough or dried fully starts to smell. Ask your laundry how hot it washes and how it dries, then order the towel that fits that laundry — not the towel that fits a laundry you wish you had. Confirm the care limits at inquiry before you order in bulk.

2. Sizes by treatment, not by the room

A spa needs four sizes, and each one has a job. The face towel is for the face and for wiping hands during a treatment — small, light, easy to fold. The hand towel is for the therapist's hands and for the guest after washing — the workhorse of the room. The body towel wraps the guest on the treatment bed — the largest towel in the room, and the one the guest judges most. The wrap or sarong is for the guest walking between the changing room, the sauna and the treatment room.

Do not order one size for the whole spa. A body towel used as a face towel wastes laundry, water and drying time on every wash. A face towel used as a body towel leaves the guest cold and uncovered. Order each size for its own job, and count them separately — the hand towel will always run out first, because it is used most.

Exact sizes and weights differ between makers, so confirm them at inquiry rather than ordering from a size chart alone. Send the supplier your treatment list — massage, facial, body scrub, sauna — and ask which sizes it recommends for each one. Our hotel towel sizes guide explains how hotel towel sizes work in general; the spa set page shows the matched set we supply for treatment rooms.

3. Thickness: soft, but not so thick it never dries

A thicker towel feels softer and more luxurious. It also takes longer to dry, costs more to wash, and weighs more in the laundry. A thinner towel dries fast and washes cheap, but feels thin on the skin. The spa towel sits between the two: thick enough to feel generous, thin enough to dry between treatments.

The practical test is the drying test, not the shelf test. A towel that feels wonderful dry in the showroom and is still damp at the next appointment is the wrong towel. Ask for a sample, wash it the way your laundry washes, and time how long it takes to dry fully. If it is not dry by the next session, order a lighter towel or order more stock so the rotation covers the drying time.

Thickness is described by weight per square metre, and the number alone tells you little without the yarn and the weave behind it. Two towels with the same weight can feel very different. Judge the sample in your hand, not the number on the page — and confirm the weight range at inquiry once the sample passes the hand test.

4. Colour: white shows cleanliness, colour hides stains

White is the spa standard for one reason: the guest can see it is clean. A white towel that is properly washed looks fresh every time, and any mark on it is visible at once — which is exactly what a guest wants to see in a spa. White also survives hot washing and bleaching better than colour, which matters when the laundry runs hot every day.

Colour has its place too. A coloured towel hides oil marks and treatment stains that white would show, and it can carry the spa's own look — a deep green, a warm sand, a soft grey. But colour fades with hot washing, and a faded towel looks old even when it is clean. If you order colour, ask how the colour holds up to your laundry temperature and confirm the colourfastness at inquiry before the bulk order.

Whichever you choose, order the whole set in the same colour. A treatment room with three shades of white looks untidy, and a guest notices. Keep one spare colour for the sauna or the pool if you want a visual difference between areas — but keep each area consistent inside itself.

5. How many to order, and how to order them

Count by treatment, not by room. A treatment room doing six treatments a day needs at least six body towels, twice as many hand towels, and a stack of face towels — plus the same again in the laundry, and a third set on the shelf for the days the laundry runs late. That rotation is the real order quantity, and it is always larger than the first guess. Our par-stock guide explains the rotation in full.

Order in sets, not in single sizes. A matched set — face, hand and body in the same colour and the same feel — keeps the treatment room consistent and the laundry simple. Single-size top-ups are for replacing worn towels later, not for the first order.

Custom work is straightforward: an embroidered logo on the body towel, a woven label, or a colour matched to the spa. Logo work needs a sample approval first — check the logo size and position on the sample before the bulk run, because a logo that looks fine on paper can sit wrong on a folded towel. Minimum order is wholesale only, US$500 excluding samples, and custom colours carry a per-colour minimum — confirm both at inquiry.

FAQ

Are spa towels the same as bath towels? No. A spa towel is washed after every treatment, meets oils and creams, and must stay soft through many more washes. A bath towel is washed once a day and never meets treatment oils. Order them separately.

Which size do I need most of? Hand towels. They are used by the therapist and the guest in every treatment, so they run out first. Count hand towels at twice the number of body towels when you plan the rotation.

White or coloured towels for a spa? White shows cleanliness and survives hot washing best. Colour hides stains and carries the spa's look, but fades faster. Most spas order white for the treatment room and keep colour for the sauna or pool areas.

How do I stop spa towels smelling of oil? Wash hot enough for your laundry to lift oils, dry fully before folding, and do not leave damp towels in a pile. If a towel still smells after washing, the wash temperature or the drying is the problem, not the towel — ask your laundry before you blame the product.

Can I get my spa logo on the towels? Yes — embroidery or a woven label, on the body towel or across the set. Approve the logo on a sample first, and confirm the per-colour minimum and the lead time at inquiry.
