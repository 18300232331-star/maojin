Terry vs Waffle vs Velour Towels: Which Weave to Order for Your Hotel

2026-11-25 · Product Knowledge

For the guest bathroom, order loop-pile terry: it holds the most water and feels the plushest, which is what guests judge. Waffle weave dries 40–50% faster and weighs roughly half as much at the same size, so it belongs in the kitchen, in spa wraps and in gym issue — not in the shower. Terry velour is terry with one face sheared smooth: it absorbs a little less on that face, but it is the best surface for a printed or embroidered logo.

1. What the three weaves are

Terry is a three-yarn cloth. A ground warp and filling form the base fabric, and a separate pile warp is woven in loops that stand proud of the ground on both sides. Those loops — their height, density and twist — are what a GSM figure mostly describes: more and taller loops mean more cotton volume available to hold water, and a deeper hand. Published trade guides put terry loops at 3–5 mm tall.

Waffle weave has no pile at all. Warp and filling float over each other in a fixed pattern, pulling the fabric into a three-dimensional grid of honeycomb cells. Those cells do two jobs: they open capillary channels that wick liquid in and spread it across the cloth, and they hold the fabric away from itself so air can move through it — which is why a waffle towel sheds water quickly and then dries much faster than a terry towel of the same size.

Terry velour starts life as terry. During finishing, a shearing machine runs across one face and cuts the top 10–20% of the pile loops, cropping them to a short, even nap. The other face keeps its loops. So one side is smooth and velvety, the other side is looped like standard terry — and the looped back still does most of the drying. That two-face build is the whole point of velour: the smooth face looks refined and takes print and embroidery cleanly, while the looped face keeps the towel working.

2. The numbers: GSM, absorbency and drying speed

Weave is not a specification on its own — it has to be ordered with a weight. Published buying guides give the usual commercial bands for each weave, and the bands barely overlap, which is why the weaves behave so differently in the laundry.

Two cautions belong with this table. First, the GSM bands are the usual commercial ranges, not hard boundaries — both weaves can be made heavier or lighter to order, and a 400 GSM waffle spa wrap is a different product from a 200 GSM waffle tea towel wearing the same name. Second, absorbency and GSM move together inside one weave but not across weaves: a heavy waffle towel still holds less water than a terry towel of the same weight, because loops store water and cells only spread it. 3. Drying time, weight and laundry cost

The weave decision is also a laundry decision, and the arithmetic is what makes it a buying decision rather than a taste decision.

Start with weight. A standard bath towel on this site is 70×140 cm, which is 0.98 m² of cloth. At 600 GSM that towel weighs 588 g; at 400 GSM it weighs 392 g — a 196 g difference, one-third lighter, before it has absorbed a drop of water. (Calculation from the published GSM bands and the site's standard size.) A waffle tea towel weighs roughly half of an equivalent-size terry hand towel, and its tumble time falls by a similar order, because the water the dryer has to evaporate simply is not there. A 400 GSM waffle towel also folds to about 40% of the volume of a 600 GSM terry towel, so it takes less shelf space, less carton space and less freight volume per piece.

Drying energy scales with the water the cloth holds. Published guides put waffle at 40–50% faster drying than terry, and one spa sourcing guide puts it at up to 40% faster. Across thousands of laundry cycles that difference compounds into measurably lower drying energy per piece — and the flat waffle face sheds almost no lint, so dryer filters and wash water stay cleaner. Terry, by contrast, sheds lint in its early washes, and its loops snag and abrade, so hems fail first.

The trade-off must be stated as honestly as the saving. A guest who used one terry bath towel may need the waffle alternative changed more often, or issued twice — which moves par stock, laundering frequency and front-of-house perception. The weave that is cheaper to run can quietly become the more expensive programme if it doubles the change frequency in the room it was misassigned to. Work out the change frequency first, then the laundry saving. Our wholesale hotel towel sets par stock guide shows how to count pieces per room and pars behind them.

No single weave wins for every towel in a hotel. Match the weave to the job the towel does, and to how often it is washed.

Sizes do not change with the weave: a bath towel is still 70×140 cm and a bath sheet 80×160 cm in our hotel towel sizes guide. What changes is how that size behaves after 50 washes — and that is the number to ask about before ordering, not after the first delivery.

5. What to put in your inquiry

Write the weave down the same way you would write the size. An inquiry that says "hotel bath towels" leaves the mill free to supply any weave; an inquiry that says "terry loop pile, 500–600 GSM, 70×140 cm, ring-spun cotton, bath towel, hotel laundry" can be quoted, sampled and checked. For waffle, add the cell size and weave density — a 200 GSM waffle tea towel and a 320 GSM waffle spa wrap are different products wearing the same name. For velour, say which face carries the logo, because the sheared face is the one that prints and embroiders cleanly: the flat nap holds ink and stitches evenly, so fine detail survives, while the looped back still does the drying.

Ask for the absorption and shrinkage figures from the actual production lot, not datasheet values from a similar cloth, and ask for the wash-fastness and rubbing-fastness results with the sample — fastness results vary by shade and construction, so ask for the results rather than a single number. Wash the sample several times in your own laundry before approving bulk production: a towel that feels perfect on arrival can change after five washes, and that is the version your guests will actually use.

Frequently asked questions

Which weave absorbs the most water? Terry. Its loop pile stores substantially more water than a waffle's cells can hold — up to 7–8 times its own weight in water — and laboratory tests report terry absorbing 30–50% more water than comparable velour of the same weight and size. Waffle absorbs quickly on contact but has lower total capacity, which is why it suits wiping and wrapping tasks rather than full-body drying.

What GSM should a waffle towel be? Commercial waffle towels usually fall between 180 and 350 GSM: lighter weights for tea and kitchen service, heavier for spa wraps and robes. Buying guides for waffle towels put Turkish cotton waffle at 280–450 GSM and recommend targeting 350+ GSM for a bath towel. State the GSM, yarn and cell size on the order rather than buying by the word "waffle" alone.

Are waffle towels good for the guest bathroom? Usually no — guests expect the capacity and plushness of terry there, especially in cooler climates. In warm climates or design-led properties, waffle bath towels can work, but they should be specified heavier and the change frequency increased to cover the capacity gap.

Is velour less absorbent than terry? A little, on the sheared face. Shearing cuts the top 10–20% of the pile loops on one side, so the velour face absorbs more slowly — but a two-sided terry velour towel still dries well because the looped back keeps its full surface. Velour earns its place where the smooth face matters: printed logos, embroidery and robes.

What should I write in my inquiry? The weave (terry, waffle or terry velour), the GSM band, the yarn, the size, the quantity per colour and the use — guest bathroom, spa, pool, gym or kitchen.
